My Experience Learning Programming and AI

Progress rarely looks perfect. It is built from small experiments, confusing errors, and the patience to try again.

September 2026•6 min read•Programming

Introduction

When I first started learning programming, many concepts seemed difficult and confusing. Programming requires a different way of thinking because a computer follows instructions exactly as they are written. A person may understand the intention behind an instruction, but a program only understands its precise logic.

Over time, I realized that programming is not mainly about memorizing commands. It is about learning how to divide a large problem into smaller problems and solve them step by step. More recently, artificial intelligence has also become an important part of my learning process.

Learning the basics

Like many students, I started with fundamental programming concepts: variables, conditions, loops, functions, arrays, and simple algorithms. At first, even small errors could take a long time to find. Sometimes the entire program failed because of one missing symbol, an incorrect variable name, or a condition that never became true.

This was frustrating, but debugging helped me understand how programs actually work. Each error forced me to read the code more carefully, inspect values, and test assumptions. Every mistake became a small lesson that was easier to remember than a definition from a textbook.

From theory to projects

Programming became more interesting when I started applying it to practical projects. A university exercise may demonstrate one concept, but a real project combines many skills at once. It can require programming, data processing, interface design, testing, error analysis, and documentation.

Projects showed me why the concepts taught at university matter. They also showed me that programmers constantly learn new tools. There is rarely one perfect solution, and even a working program can often be made clearer, safer, or more efficient.

Discovering artificial intelligence

Artificial intelligence added another interesting area to my studies. At first, AI seemed extremely complicated because it involved programming, mathematics, data, and algorithms. Learning the basic ideas gradually made the subject easier to understand.

One important lesson was that AI systems are not magic. They are built from algorithms, data, mathematical models, and computing resources. Understanding this changed the way I viewed modern AI applications. Instead of treating every output as intelligent or correct, I began to ask how the system might have produced it and what limitations it might have.

Using AI while learning programming

AI assistants can be useful when learning programming. When I receive an error, I can ask a system to explain what the message means. Instead of requesting only corrected code, I prefer an explanation that describes:

  1. where the problem is;
  2. why it happens;
  3. how it can be fixed;
  4. how to avoid the same mistake later.

This approach helps me learn instead of simply copying a solution. AI is also useful when reading unfamiliar code. It can explain the purpose of each section and provide a simpler example that isolates one concept.

My learning habit: first attempt the task independently, then use AI for a hint or explanation, and finally rewrite the solution in my own way.

Challenges along the way

One of the biggest challenges in programming is staying patient. Sometimes a solution appears correct but still does not work. Finding the issue may require testing several ideas and accepting that the first assumption was wrong.

Another challenge is the amount of information available online. There are hundreds of programming languages, frameworks, libraries, and AI technologies. It is easy to feel that everything must be learned immediately. I found it more effective to focus on fundamental concepts and learn new tools only when a project requires them.

Skills beyond programming

Learning technology has also helped me develop skills that are useful outside coding: problem-solving, logical thinking, research, patience, independent learning, and attention to detail. Technology changes quickly, so the ability to learn independently may be more valuable than knowledge of one specific tool.

Conclusion

My experience learning programming and artificial intelligence has shown me that progress comes from practice. Making mistakes is a normal part of learning and often the moment when real understanding begins.

Artificial intelligence can make this process faster by explaining concepts and helping identify problems. However, students still need to think independently and understand the solutions they use. In the future, I want to continue developing my programming and AI skills and apply them in larger practical projects.
